How to Password Protect a PDF File — Free Method

📅 June 2026 ⏱️ 3 min read ✍️ Ahmad — Founder, EggyPDF
← Back to Blog

Sending sensitive documents by email always carries a risk. Password protecting your PDF adds an extra layer of security — the recipient needs the password to open the file, even if the email is intercepted or forwarded unintentionally.

When Should You Password Protect a PDF?

  • Sending salary slips, bank statements, or financial records
  • Sharing contracts or legal agreements
  • Sending personal identification documents
  • Distributing confidential business reports
  • Protecting exam papers or proprietary content

How to Add Password Protection for Free

  2. Upload your PDF file
  3. Enter the password you want to set
  4. Download your password-protected PDF

Share the password separately from the file — send it by SMS or phone call rather than in the same email as the attachment.

Choosing a Strong Password

  • Use at least 8 characters
  • Mix uppercase letters, numbers, and symbols
  • Avoid obvious passwords like your name or date of birth
  • Never use the same password you use for your email or bank

How is This Different from Encryption?

Password protection restricts who can open the file. Encryption scrambles the content so it cannot be read even if the file is accessed. EggyPDF's tool applies both — the PDF is encrypted using AES-256 standard, which is the same encryption level used by banks.
